Newsletters you read on your schedule, not theirs.

2026-08-26 · guide · filters and archiving

The goal isn't deleting newsletters — it's that arriving stops being an event. Archived-with-a-label mail is still searchable, still readable over coffee, and costs zero attention at 2pm on a Tuesday. Here's the clean setup, and the one mistake that makes people abandon it.

The two-minute version

  1. Open a newsletter → ⋮ → Filter messages like this. Match the sender (or their domain).
  2. Create the filter with: Skip the Inbox (Archive it) + Apply the label: Newsletters.
  3. Optionally tick "Apply to matching conversations" to sweep the backlog.

Repeat per sender as they arrive. Within two weeks the inbox is quiet and the Newsletters label is a reading queue you visit on purpose.

The mistake: keyword filters

The tempting shortcut is one broad filter on "unsubscribe" — since nearly all bulk mail contains the word. So do plenty of receipts, booking confirmations and account notices. A keyword archive-filter fails silently: the mail isn't gone, but nothing tells you it arrived, and you find out when the renewal you missed was in the archive all along. Filter on senders, not words. Slower to build, and it never eats a receipt.

Keep a safety net

Once a week, glance at the label for anything misfiled — a human replying from a newsletter address, an invoice from a sender you archived. Sender-based filters make this rare; the glance is what makes it safe.

What this looks like with triage instead of filters

The setup above is a hand-built version of one lane. Klorn ships it as behaviour: clear marketing goes to a silent lane on its own, with the reason attached, and two design choices close this guide's failure modes. Corrections and read behaviour can rescue a sender from silence — a newsletter you actually open stops being buried — but nothing learned can ever silence a sender on its own; that direction is deliberately blocked. And the safety-net glance has a place to live: silenced rows still exist, visible when you look, never rendered when you don't.
