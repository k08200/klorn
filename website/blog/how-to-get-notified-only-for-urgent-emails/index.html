Get notified only for email that matters.

2026-08-26 · guide · Gmail on Android, iOS and desktop

Email notifications ship in exactly two configurations: every message buzzes, or nothing does. Both are wrong — the first trains you to ignore your phone, the second makes you check it constantly in case something urgent landed. Here are the four ways to get a middle setting that actually works, including what each one cannot do.

1. Gmail's built-in “High priority only”

The fastest fix, and most people have never opened the menu it lives in.

  1. In the Gmail app, open Settings and pick the account.
  2. Tap Notifications and choose High priority only (Android and iOS both have it).

Google's importance model now decides what buzzes. It is genuinely decent at obvious cases — a reply from someone you email often will get through, a newsletter won't.

Where it falls short: the decision is a black box. When something important stays silent — and it will — Gmail never tells you why it judged the message unimportant, and your only lever is toggling the tiny importance marker and hoping the model notices. There is also no equivalent on desktop, and no notion of “urgent” as distinct from “important”: a message from your co-founder about lunch and one about the production database being down rank the same.

2. Filters + per-label notifications (Android)

The most control Gmail itself will give you:

  1. On the web, create a filter (Show search options → Create filter) that matches your must-hear-about mail — specific senders, your domain, “invoice”, whatever — and apply a label such as Urgent.
  2. In the Android app: Settings → account → Notifications → Manage labels, turn notifications off for the inbox and on for Urgent.

Where it falls short: you are now the classifier. Every new sender that matters is a rule you didn't write yet, every rule you did write goes stale, and the failure mode is silent — mail that matches no rule just doesn't buzz. iOS doesn't support per-label notifications, so the setup is Android-only.

3. Desktop: important-only browser notifications

On the web, Settings → General → Desktop notifications → Important mail notifications on. Same importance model as method 1, same opacity, but it at least extends the behaviour to your computer while Gmail is open in a tab.

4. The phone-level blunt instrument

iOS Focus modes and Android's notification channels can silence the Gmail app on a schedule or allow it through Do Not Disturb. Useful as a backstop, but they filter the app, not the mail — inside the allowed window everything still buzzes equally.

If you want zero setup, take method 1 and accept the black box. If your important mail is predictable — a boss, a domain, an alerts address — method 2 is the most reliable thing on this page and costs you a maintenance habit.

The structural gap all four share

Each method answers “should this buzz?” with either an opaque model or a hand-written rule. None of them can tell you why a message was judged the way it was, and none of them treat a miss as something you can see and correct.

That gap is the reason Klorn exists. It gives every inbound message exactly one of five lanes — only the urgent lane interrupts you, scheduling mail gets a calendar cross-check, and everything else waits visibly in a queue. The difference from the methods above is not a smarter model; it is that every decision carries its reason (“you reply to this sender”, “clear marketing”), a wrong lane is fixed by moving the row — which is exactly what the classifier trains on — and the failure direction is pinned by a CI-enforced test: a missed urgent email degrades to the visible queue, never to the hidden lane. It's AGPL and self-hostable, so none of this requires trusting a black box either.
